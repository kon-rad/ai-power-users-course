## 1. The spine (one line)

> **We bring a proven, open-source, already-running AI education program to Cambodia and
> teach a room of your people, one-on-one, to become confident private-AI power users in
> five days — and leave the entire curriculum behind, free and open, so it keeps running
> after we fly home.**

This is not a conference talk or a demo tour. It is a **capability transfer**: at the end of
the week your people can *do the work themselves*, and Cambodia owns the materials forever.

---

## 2. Why this, why now

- **AI capability is the new literacy — and it's a sovereignty issue.** The gap between
  countries that can *use* AI and those that only consume it is opening fast. A week of
  hands-on training that leaves behind an open curriculum is one of the cheapest, highest-
  leverage moves a government can make in that race.
- **Private-by-default fits national interest.** The whole program is built around AI that
  runs **locally, privately, and where possible fully offline** — no dependence on a single
  foreign cloud, no sensitive data leaving the country. That aligns with digital-sovereignty
  and data-protection goals better than any off-the-shelf vendor course.
- **It already exists and already runs.** This is not a concept. The course is written,
  live-streamed on YouTube, delivered to a real cohort, open-sourced, and continuously
  evolving. You are funding an execution, not an experiment.
- **Calendar leverage.** End of August is ~3–4 weeks out. Committing now secures dates,
  travel, and venue while they're still cheap and available — a real reason to close in the
  meeting rather than "circle back."

---

## 3. What Cambodia actually gets (the differentiator)

The obvious objection is *"there are free AI courses on YouTube — why fund a trip?"* Here's
the answer — everything below is impossible to get from a video:

- **One-on-one, in-person mentorship.** Online, this is a peer cohort. In the room, it
  becomes **high-touch, individual coaching** — we sit with each learner, unblock them on
  their own machine, and don't move on until it works. That is the difference between people
  who *watched* a course and people who can *do the work*.
- **A finished, working stack on every laptop.** By Friday each participant has a private AI
  second brain, prompting fluency, multimodal skills, RAG over their own documents, one real
  automation, and a self-hosted/offline model — all installed, wired together, and running.
- **The full curriculum, left behind, free and open source.** Every module, script, student
  guide, quiz, and rubric stays with Cambodia under an open license. **Nobody is locked into
  us.** Your teachers can re-run it next month without paying anyone.
- **Train-the-trainer built in.** We can bias the week so a core group can *teach it
  themselves* afterward — turning a one-week visit into a repeatable national asset.
- **Local visibility and content.** Live-streamed sessions, a public showcase, and a demo
  day produce shareable proof that Cambodia is investing in real AI capability — content for
  the sponsor's channels and a flag on the initiative.
- **Private-first, sovereignty-aligned tooling.** Free and open-source tools throughout;
  data stays on local machines; the self-hosting module shows how to run AI with no foreign
  cloud dependency at all.

### The value math (say this out loud)
A single vendor-run "AI upskilling" workshop from a consultancy runs **tens of thousands of
dollars for one delivery — and you own nothing afterward.** Here, for the cost of one trip
and a facilitation fee, you get the delivery **plus the open curriculum forever, plus a
trained local group who can re-run it.** The second and third cohorts cost Cambodia
approximately **zero.** That is the whole point.

## 4. The program (what we deliver)

**Format:** 5 days, ~3 hours/day of core instruction (in-person we extend with hands-on lab
and one-on-one coaching time), 15 modules, hands-on every session, ending in a capstone each
participant builds and presents. Full map in [`course-overview.md`](./course-overview.md).

| Day | Theme | Outcome by end of day |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | **Your Private AI Second Brain** | A working private AI agent + note system on your own machine |
| Tue | **Prompting & Talking to Models** | Fluency with prompts and system prompts across models |
| Wed | **Beyond Text: Multimodal** | Generate and edit image, video, audio, voice; transcription + local-language AI |
| Thu | **Your Data & Automation** | Chat with your own documents (RAG) and automate one real, repetitive task |
| Fri | **Privacy, Self-Hosting & Capstone** | Run a model fully offline; ship + present one real project |

**Adapted for Cambodia:**
- **One-on-one coaching blocks** replace some of the online peer-lab time.
- **Local-language & cultural localization** (Day 3, Module 8) — transcription, translation,
  and localization for Khmer, made concrete with participants' own material.
- **Optional train-the-trainer track** for a designated group of future instructors.
- **Public showcase / demo day** the sponsor can invite press and officials to.

**Culture of the room** (the six values — see overview): win-and-help-win, create more than
you consume, done over perfect, participate. It's a practice, not a lecture.

## 5. Why the Argo brand (the shift)

We are moving this program from the **Global Builders Club** banner to **Argo** — the private
AI journaling app (myargo.quest). This is deliberate and it strengthens the pitch:

- **The brand *is* the mission.** Argo stands for **lifelong education, self-reliance, private
  AI, and open-source software.** A course that teaches exactly those values belongs under
  that flag, not a generic community label.
- **A coherent story.** *Argo* is the ship that carries a bold crew on a hard, shared quest
  toward a valuable prize (see [`../renaming-to-argo-pros-cons.md`](../renaming-to-argo-pros-cons.md)).
  Cambodia's learners become **Argonauts** on that voyage — a clean, memorable identity for a
  national cohort.
- **Something real behind it.** Argo isn't a slogan; it's a working product built on the same
  private-by-default, own-your-data principles the course teaches. That makes the instructor
  credible: this is how I actually build.
- **No lock-in conflict.** The *course* stays free and open source regardless of brand. Argo
  is the philosophy and the home, not a paywall.

## 7. Proposed compensation structure (for Konrad)

Two clean ways to frame it. Lead with Option A; Option B is the fallback if the host balks at
a flat fee.

### Option A — Trip + flat facilitation fee (recommended)
- **All trip costs covered** (flights, lodging, local transport, per diem, venue) — paid or
  pre-booked by the host, not reimbursed weeks later.
- **Facilitation fee: USD $5,000–$8,000** for the 5-day intensive.
  - Anchoring: a private consultancy charges tens of thousands for a single non-transferable
    delivery. This fee buys the delivery **plus the open curriculum handover plus optional
    train-the-trainer** — still a fraction of the alternative.
  - Frame it as **~$1,000–$1,600/day of expert instruction**, which is modest for hands-on,
    one-on-one AI training.

### Option B — Trip + per-seat fee (scales with cohort)
- **All trip costs covered**, plus **USD $150–$300 per participant**, with a **minimum
  guarantee** (e.g. floor of $4,000) so a small pilot cohort still makes the trip worthwhile.
- Good when the host is funding seats rather than a program, or wants the number tied to
  reach.

### Add-ons (either option)
- **Train-the-trainer track:** +USD $1,500–$3,000 (extra prep + a dedicated instructor day).
- **Repeat / additional cohorts** in the same trip: +USD $2,500–$4,000 per additional week.
- **Ongoing advisory retainer** after the visit (async support as they re-run it): optional
  USD $500–$1,000/month, month-to-month.

### Konrad's negotiation floor (internal — do not show the host)
- **Walk-away line:** all trip costs fully covered **is non-negotiable** — never be out of
  pocket to teach. On top of that, a facilitation fee of **at least ~$3,000–$4,000** for a
  full week of your time. Below that, it's not worth the week away from Argo.
- **Target:** trip covered + **$6,000ish** fee + venue provided by host.
- **Currency & payment:** ask for USD, with **50% before departure** (secures flights/dates)
  and 50% on completion. Avoid full-on-completion terms.

## 9. Risks & how we handle them

- **"YouTube is free."** → Answer with §3: one-on-one coaching, a finished stack on every
  machine, and the open curriculum they *own*. Video doesn't unblock you at 4pm when your
  install breaks.
- **Timeline is tight.** → Lean into it as urgency, not risk: the course already exists, so
  prep is packaging + localization, not building. Confirm venue + connectivity early.
- **Connectivity / power.** → Modules are designed to degrade gracefully; the self-hosting
  module runs **fully offline**, which is a feature, not a workaround.
- **Language.** → Day 3 covers translation/localization directly; bring a local co-facilitator
  or translator if the cohort needs it (fold into budget).
- **Sustainability after we leave.** → Train-the-trainer + open curriculum means the program
  survives our departure. That's the headline, not an afterthought.
