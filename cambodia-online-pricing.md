## TL;DR — the five things that matter

1. **The free ticket costs you a third of the deal, and it changes the price.** With 2 payers instead of 3, every per-person price yields **33% less**. To keep the same money per class you'd need to charge **1.5× more per head** — and their ceiling won't stretch that far. So you can't fully recover it on price. Recover it in kind (§4) or accept the smaller number knowingly.
2. **Ask $40/person/class. Land at $30–35. Floor at $25.** With two payers: **$80 → $60–70 → $50 per class**, and **$320 → $240–280 → $200** across the four weeks.
3. **Most likely to change the plan — make the free seat earn its keep.** A free seat at this scale is worth $120–160 of your revenue. It should be conditional, not a gift: it buys the **testimonial, the recording consent, and one more paying person recruited.** "Your seat is free if you bring someone" is the single highest-return sentence available to you — one extra payer at $30 restores everything the free ticket took.
4. **Charge the free seat something nominal — $5 or $10 a class.** Not for the money. In a three-person room the unpaid seat is the most likely no-show, and one absence is a third of the class. People show up for what they paid for.
5. **The honest bottom line: $240–320 for ~12 hours of your time is $20–27/hour.** This is not revenue, it's a pilot. It's worth doing for the recordings, the case study, and the upsell — and it stops being worth doing the moment they refuse those (§5).

## 1. The ceiling you're pricing against

**Known:** they would not pay **$80 for a 3-day workshop at 3 hours/day** (9 contact hours) — about **$8.89/hour**.

That's a ceiling on **total outlay**, not on hourly rate. $8.89/hour is cheap by any measure; nobody rejects that because the rate is too high. They rejected the lump. Which is exactly why per-class pricing is the right instinct — it breaks $80-at-once into four small decisions.

**But:** the moment you say "so that's $160 for the four," you have rebuilt the lump they rejected. **Never present the total unprompted.**

### What they pay per hour, compared to what they rejected

| What | Their total | Their contact hours | **Their cost per hour** |
|---|---|---|---|
| The workshop they rejected | $80 | 9 | **$8.89** |
| 4 classes at $20 | $80 | 8 | $10.00 |
| 4 classes at $25 | $100 | 8 | $12.50 |
| **4 classes at $30** | **$120** | 8 | **$15.00** |
| 4 classes at $35 | $140 | 8 | $17.50 |
| 4 classes at $40 | $160 | 8 | $20.00 |
| 4 classes at $50 | $200 | 8 | $25.00 |

**$30/class is the sweet spot in this table.** $15/hour against the $8.89 they walked away from is a **69% increase per hour** — defensible, because the format goes from a 9-hour group workshop to four sessions with three people and live help on their own machine. **$40/class asks 2.25× per hour** what they already refused: possible, but know the size of that jump before you open there.

## 2. The math with only 2 payers

Your committed time: **~2h live + ~1h prep/async = 3 hours per class, 12 hours total.**

| Per person/class | Payers | **Your per class** | **Your 4-class total** | **Your $/hour** | Each payer's 4-week total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $20 | 2 | $40 | $160 | $13.33 | $80 |
| **$25** (floor) | 2 | **$50** | **$200** | **$16.67** | $100 |
| **$30** (land) | 2 | **$60** | **$240** | **$20.00** | $120 |
| **$35** (land) | 2 | **$70** | **$280** | **$23.33** | $140 |
| **$40** (ask) | 2 | **$80** | **$320** | **$26.67** | $160 |
| $50 | 2 | $100 | $400 | $33.33 | $200 |
| $60 | 2 | $120 | $480 | $40.00 | $240 |

### What the free ticket actually costs

| | 3 payers | 2 payers | Cost of the free seat |
|---|---|---|---|
| At $30/class | $90/class · $360 total | $60/class · $240 total | **−$120 (−33%)** |
| At $40/class | $120/class · $480 total | $80/class · $320 total | **−$160 (−33%)** |

To hold **$120/class** with two payers you would need **$60/person** — $240 each over the month, **3× the $80 they rejected.** That is not reachable. **Conclusion: the free seat cannot be recovered through price.** Either recover it in kind (§4), or accept a $240–320 deal with your eyes open.

### The bundle option, recalculated

The 3-for-4 offer ("pay for three classes, the fourth is free") now stacks a second discount on top of the free seat:

| Offer | Each payer pays | **Your 4-class total (2 payers)** | Your $/hour |
|---|---|---|---|
| $40/class, no bundle | $160 | **$320** | $26.67 |
| $40/class, 3-for-4 bundle | $120 | **$240** | $20.00 |
| $30/class, no bundle | $120 | **$240** | $20.00 |
| $30/class, 3-for-4 bundle | $90 | **$180** | $15.00 |

**Notice the middle two rows are identical for you — $240 — but the bundle asks each payer for $120 either way.** So the bundle buys you nothing on price; its only value is **getting one payment upfront instead of eight small transfers** (§6). Given you're already down a third from the free ticket, **use the bundle only if transfer friction is real** — otherwise just charge $40/class and keep the $320.

## 3. Recommended offer

> **$40 per person per class.** Four classes, two hours each. **If transfers are a hassle, $120 for all four paid upfront.**
> **The third seat is free — on the condition it comes with a written testimonial, permission to record, and one more person in the room.**

| Level | Per person/class | Your per class | Your total | When |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ask** | **$40** | $80 | $320 | Open here |
| **Target landing** | **$30–35** | $60–70 | $240–280 | Most likely close given the $80 signal |
| **Floor** | **$25** | $50 | $200 | Only with the §5 terms granted in writing |
| **Walk away** | below $25 | under $50 | under $200 | $200 for 12 hours is the edge. Below it, decline and point at the free YouTube cohort |

**If a third payer appears, drop everyone to $30.** Three payers at $30 is $90/class — better than two at $40 — and it gives you a bigger room, a better recording, and three case studies instead of two. Say this out loud to them; it's the cheapest recruiting incentive you have.

## 4. The free seat — turn it from a gift into a lever

A free ticket for four classes is **$120–160 of foregone revenue at these prices.** Never hand that over unconditionally. Attach one or more:

| Condition | What it's worth |
|---|---|
| **"Free if you bring one more paying person"** | **The best of these by far.** One recruit at $30 = +$120, which fully cancels the free seat |
| **They provide the written testimonial and case study** | The credential that sells the next cohort |
| **They consent to recording and publishing** | Four sessions of content |
| **Free for 2 of the 4 classes, not all 4** | Halves the cost, still reads as generous |
| **Nominal $5–10/class instead of $0** | Attendance insurance, not revenue — see below |

**On the nominal fee:** in a three-person room, the unpaid seat is the one most likely to skip, arrive late, or quietly drop. One absence is a third of your class and it wrecks the peer dynamic you have left. **$10 a class is not about the $40 — it's about the person showing up.** If the free ticket is already promised and you can't attach a price, at least attach an obligation: they present at the mini-capstone, or they write the testimonial.

## 5. Non-cash terms — at $240 these *are* the payment

| Term | Why it's worth more than the fee |
|---|---|
| **Recording + publishing rights** | Four sessions of course material and YouTube content, plus proof the program runs |
| **Named case study + testimonials** | "Delivered to a Cambodian cohort" is what sells the next one |
| **Letter of support / named reference** | Directly revives the in-person pitch (§8 of `cambodia-pitch.md`) |
| **Two warm intros** | Other orgs, universities, or ministries in the region |
| **Recruit more people** | Every extra payer is worth more than any concession you'd win on price |
| **Credit toward the full program** | 100% of what they pay credits toward the full 15-module program within 6 months. Costs nothing at this size; turns $120 into the opening move on a real deal |

**Decision rule: if they won't grant recording rights and testimonials, the price goes up, not down** — or you decline. Without those, $240 for 12 hours is simply a bad trade, and there's no version of this where the cash alone justifies the month.

## 6. Payment mechanics

At $60–80 per class from two people, **international transfer fees can eat a real slice, eight times over.** Sort this before you quote.

- **Nominate one coordinator** to collect locally and send once. Eight small cross-border payments at this size is absurd.
- **This is the only good reason to use the bundle** — one transfer instead of eight, at a cost of $80 to you (§2). Worth it if their rails are painful, not otherwise.
- **Rail:** Wise, or whatever they already use (ABA / Wing are common in Cambodia). **Confirm what they can actually send internationally before agreeing terms** — this is where small cross-border deals die.
- If transfers are genuinely painful, consider collecting when you're next in Cambodia (see `Projects/cambodia-trip/`).
- **Never pay-on-completion.** Prepaid per class, or the bundle.

## 7. What the money buys — 8 hours, scoped honestly

Four classes covers about 4 of the 15 modules. Pick the ones that leave a **working thing on their laptop**.

| Week | Modules | They walk away with |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | 1 + 2 — Agent & Private AI Second Brain Setup | The private-AI stack installed and running, PARA wired up, first agent skill working |
| **2** | 4 + 5 — How Models Work · Prompting & System Prompts | Fluency with prompts and system prompts |
| **3** | 10 + 11 — RAG · Agents, Tools & Automation | Chatting with their own documents, plus one real task automated |
| **4** | 13 + mini-capstone | Credential/privacy hygiene, then ship and present one real thing |

**Offer one elective swap** — trade week 3 or 4 for **Module 8, Speech-to-Text & Multi-Language AI (Khmer transcription, translation, localisation)**. Costs nothing, most locally relevant hour in the course, and a choice lands better than a discount.

**Out of scope, and say so:** multimodal generation (7), self-hosting/offline (14), the peer-lab track, bespoke development. These are the reasons to buy more later.

## 8. Handling the price conversation

| They say | You do |
|---|---|
| "That's more than the $80 workshop" | "That was nine hours in a room with everyone. This is four sessions with three of you — I'm on your screen fixing your machine. And it's $40 a week, not $80 at once" |
| "Can it be cheaper?" | **"Yes — bring more people."** Three payers and it's $30 each. Five and it's $25. Make the discount something they earn, never something you concede |
| "There are free AI courses online" | **Agree completely.** The curriculum is open source and the cohort is free on YouTube. They're paying for you on the call at 8pm when the install breaks. Same answer as §9 of the pitch |
| "Can we just try one?" | **Yes — that's the point of per-class.** Pay for class 1, decide after. Never a free trial, never a discounted first class |
| "Can we pay at the end?" | No. Prepaid before each class, or the bundle |
| "Can we do 3 classes?" | Fine at the same per-class rate — but week 1 is the install week, and skipping the last one means no finished project and no capstone to film |

**Give them a genuinely free path** — YouTube plus the open repo. It's already true, and it makes the paid price a choice rather than a squeeze.

## 9. Logistics

| Item | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Timezone** | Cambodia is ICT (UTC+7); Malaysia is UTC+8 — one hour ahead. A 7pm Phnom Penh class is 8pm yours. Lock one fixed weekly slot for all four weeks up front |
| **Platform** | Whatever they already use. Don't make tooling a barrier at this price |
| **Recording** | Get consent up front, not on class 1 |
| **Machines** | Confirm before week 1 that all three have laptops that can run the stack. Week 1 is the install week and a surprise here burns the session |
| **Attendance** | Three people, one free. Say up front that the schedule is fixed and there are no make-up sessions — or you'll be teaching four classes twice |
| **Scope boundary** | Written: 4 classes, the modules in §7, async support between classes, no bespoke development |

## 10. Assumptions & open questions

**Assumed (confirm before quoting):**
- **4 classes × 2 hours = 8 contact hours**, fixed end date, no automatic continuation.
- **3 attendees, 2 paying**, and the free ticket is already committed. If it isn't yet promised, attach the §4 conditions *before* offering it.
- **The two pay personally.** If an employer or agency reimburses, throw this out and quote a program fee — an org pays several hundred for a group without blinking.
- The rejected $80 workshop was **comparable** — same audience, paid personally, AI/tech content. If it was a different crowd or subject, it's a weaker signal than this file treats it as.
- Prep stays light because the curriculum exists. **Khmer localisation of written materials is a separate line item.**

**Not verified — do not quote to them:**
- Local reference prices for comparable Phnom Penh IT/professional short courses, and typical individual willingness-to-pay in Cambodia. Everything here triangulates from **one** data point (the $80 rejection) plus your own rate. That single point is doing a lot of work.

**Open questions for Konrad:**
1. **Why does the third person have a free ticket** — a favour, a connector, the person who organised it? That decides which §4 condition you can attach without friction.
2. **Whose $80 workshop was it, and who turned it down** — these same people, or a wider group?
3. **Is the in-person week dead, deferred, or still live?** Changes whether this is a pilot or the whole relationship.
4. **Is 4 classes their cap or their opening?** If they're testing before committing, lean hard on the credit-toward-the-full-program clause.
